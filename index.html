Executive Brief

Why This Matters Now

Vispero is at a critical inflection point. With a new CEO, ELT-approved 2H 2026 initiatives, and a active private-equity holding period, every dollar spent must directly tie to clear business outcomes—not technology for its own sake.

Part 1: Framing the Problem

Opening questions designed to surface urgency and align stakeholders before presenting solutions.

Revenue Visibility

  • How confident are we in our renewal pipeline today—and what does a missed renewal cost us?
  • How long does it take to produce an accurate renewal report? Is that acceptable at our growth trajectory?
  • With renewal data split between Great Plains and Salesforce, how many manual steps slow down reps?

Operational Risk

  • What happens to quote-to-order when a customer record is missing in Great Plains?
  • With ~47 undocumented point-to-point integrations built by former contractors, what is our recovery time if one breaks?
  • Can we make ERP strategy decisions without knowing what needs rebuilding if Great Plains changes?

Data Quality & Trust

  • Do we have a single, agreed source of truth for contract end dates between Salesforce, Great Plains, and the license server?
  • Are we confident data is clean enough before migrating to the new Salesforce org?
  • How are we managing churn risk without linked customer records across key systems?

Part 2: The Business Case

Declarative, board-ready statements that frame the investment in business outcome terms.

On Revenue Protection

Centralizing renewals data and automating sync between Salesforce and Great Plains is a revenue protection play. At 35–40% identified leakage, this is the single highest-ROI initiative available to Vispero today.

On Operational Efficiency

Producing a pipeline report today takes weeks. A governed integration layer brings real-time visibility—freeing sales teams to sell, rather than reconcile spreadsheets.

On Risk Reduction

47 undocumented point-to-point connections mean system changes carry hidden risk. Replacing these with a modern, governed platform de-risks the business as we scale.

On ERP Flexibility

A "swappable fitting" integration architecture standardizes CRM-to-ERP connections, protecting us from vendor lock-in if the board decides to replace Great Plains.

On Investment & Timing

MuleSoft + Informatica packaging changes traditional $100K+ spend dynamics. Cleaning data and establishing governance now prevents inheriting technical debt at scale during org consolidation.

Part 3: Proposed Phased Investment

Phase Timing What We're Solving Expected Outcome
Crawl Jul–Sep 2026 Data quality: cleanse, dedupe, establish source-of-truth rules Accurate renewal pipeline in weeks, not months
Walk Oct–Dec 2026 System integration: connect Salesforce ↔ Great Plains ↔ License Server Real-time Q2O sync, zero manual double-entry
Run Jan–Feb 2027 AI scaling: predictive renewal alerts, risk models, agent workflows Proactive revenue retention at scale

Part 4: MuleSoft vs. Informatica — The "Why Both" Story

Tool Role Vispero Use Case
MuleSoft Real-time, event-driven app-to-app integration Salesforce ↔ Great Plains transactional sync (Q2O, account creation, pricing)
Informatica Batch data movement, governance, data quality at scale Deduplication, canonical record creation, renewals data cleanse

The Board Analogy

MuleSoft is the plumbing that moves data between systems in real time. Informatica is the water treatment plant that ensures the water moving through those pipes is clean and trustworthy. You need both.

Part 5: Anticipated Board Objections & Responses

"This sounds like a big IT project."

It's structured as a 3-phase program with measurable business outcomes at each stage. Phase 1 costs less than a single missed enterprise renewal.

"Can't we just fix this with Salesforce alone?"

Salesforce manages customer relationships; it is not designed to be a enterprise data quality engine or ERP integration hub. MuleSoft and Informatica fill that precise infrastructure gap.

"What about our ERP decision—should we wait?"

No. Waiting locks in manual risk. Our proposed architecture is explicitly ERP-agnostic and will survive a Great Plains replacement seamlessly.
